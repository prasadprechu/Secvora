## Introduction

Distributed denial-of-service attacks remain a major threat to modern digital services. Attackers can generate large volumes of malicious traffic to overwhelm networks, applications, or critical infrastructure.

## What Is a DDoS Attack?

A DDoS attack attempts to make a service unavailable by sending a large amount of traffic or requests from many compromised systems.

## Common Types of DDoS Attacks

### Volumetric Attacks

These attacks attempt to consume available network bandwidth with massive traffic volumes.

### Protocol Attacks

Protocol attacks target network and transport-layer resources, such as connection tables and network devices.

### Application-Layer Attacks

Application-layer attacks target specific applications and endpoints with large numbers of seemingly legitimate requests.

## How DDoS Protection Works

Modern DDoS protection combines traffic analysis, rate limiting, behavioral detection, traffic filtering, and automated mitigation to identify and block malicious traffic.

## Conclusion

Effective DDoS protection requires continuous monitoring and the ability to respond quickly as attack patterns change. Organizations should combine network-level controls with application-layer security for comprehensive protection.
